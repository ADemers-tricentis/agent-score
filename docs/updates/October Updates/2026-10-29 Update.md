## Key Discussion Points

1. **Why Agent Score is missing from the AI Credit Consumption Dashboard**
   - Matt asked on Tuesday. The dashboard's "exclude internal tenants" rule treats anything in the TAIS tenant as internal and ignores it. That is a different meaning of internal than Agent Score uses.
   - Lior set it up that way on purpose: Tricentis should not bill customers for Tricentis agents.
   - With no external agents connected, Agent Score has nothing the dashboard will count. Connecting an external agent should make it appear. Nobody ran that test on the call.

2. **Do not bill AI Workspace scores to the customer tenant**
   - Tim and Andrew had settled, provisionally, on this rule: if someone in the tenant has logged into Agent Score and accepted terms, take that tenant's AI Workspace traces, score them in the customer tenant, and charge credits. If they have never accepted terms, do not.
   - Lior rejected it. One person clicking through terms would start scoring and charging every trace from every user and agent in that tenant. His example was Wolters Kluwer with 100 people in AI Workspace.
   - AI credits are not being charged for this path today. Merging the change would start sending that consumption as the customer's.
   - External tenants are different. Scoring an external agent does report usage. Engineering sends the consumption events. What the commercialization service does with them, including setting the credit cost to zero, is a product decision. Engineering does not decide the amount.
   - Internal and AI Workspace usage is reported under the TAIS account. That was the point of last week's change to add the TAIS account. Tritusa has an AI Workspace agent running under the Tritusa tenant, and that usage currently rolls up to TAIS, so individual customers are not visible there.
   - Putting that usage on the customer tenant would charge them. Lior was explicit: do not do it. He is not the person to make the legal call, and he does not think it is allowed.
   - The visibility Tim wants (who is scoring, how many evals per run) is supposed to land in the insights work Lior has been building for the last two days. Internal and external both show there, without charging. The Reports tab already exposes most of the metrics.
   - There is not enough regular usage yet for those reports to say what normal looks like. A spike can just be someone clicking around after signup. Getting real users is on product.
   - Legal opt-in is the path for ingesting AI Workspace traces. It has to be the end user opting in. Legal is slow. Start per tenant. Per agent comes later, at GA, because a tenant might have a thousand AI Workspace agents and not want all of them scored.
   - Even with opt-in, AI Hub billing is a second routing problem: each action has to say which tenant to charge. External billable actions go to the customer. AI Workspace stays under TAIS. That split is why a "just attribute it" change is not small.
   - Lior's advice for the legal conversation: do not explain what is already built. Describe the flow, who uses what, and ask who gets charged and what gets dropped. Ask whether to charge at all. The amount can be 0 or 100. Do not lead with a number.
   - Product should map every action, internal and external, to charge or not charge, and hand engineering that blueprint. Not the prices. Lior asked that this map be done by hand, not generated.
   - The likely pricing shape, once there is data, is credits per eval inside a scoring run, so a 100-eval run costs more than a 10-eval run. COGS is too early. Price against value first. Trace compression (Headroom and similar) comes later.
   - Lior also does not want a charge on every new custom eval.

7. **MCP scopes, starting right after the call**
   - Some organizations will only allow the MCP if it is read-only. They will not want delete-agent, trigger-scoring, or onboard-agent (onboarding costs money). Some will not want prompts sent at all.
   - Claude Code already has per-tool block, allow, and ask. That is client-side. Lior will also enforce it on the server.
   - Back office can enable or disable MCP per tenant, and can set a scope policy per tenant or organization. A user can only narrow what the tenant already allows.
   - Scopes will be groups (read, write, and similar) plus a toggle per tool.
   - Two levels: tenant policy in the back office, and a setting in the web UI. The UI setting is for that user account, not for the whole tenant.
   - Product wants real roles inside a customer tenant later: an admin at Wolters Kluwer decides who can score, add an agent, or create an ingest key, and a regular user cannot widen their own access. Lior said that is a separate external admin surface, not this change. Build scoping now. Add in-tenant roles when it is a requirement.
   - Scoping should be usable in one to two days. He will still bring up the MCP for people to try before that lands.

## Decisions Made

- Do not attribute AI Workspace scoring to a customer tenant, and do not charge them for it, on the strength of one user accepting terms. Close that pull request. Usage for this path stays under TAIS.
- External-agent usage keeps being reported. The credit amount, including zero, is a product decision. Engineering implements a charge / do-not-charge blueprint once product writes it.
- AI Workspace traces are not ingested for customer scoring until the end user opts in. Legal has to answer that. Per tenant first. Per agent only if this reaches GA.
- When talking to legal, describe the flows and ask who to charge and what to drop. Do not brief them on the current implementation, and do not lead with a price.
- COGS and trace compression are out of scope for pricing now. Decide from value, using real usage once it exists.
- MCP tool policy will be enforced per tenant from the back office, with groups plus per-tool toggles. Users can only narrow the tenant policy. Their own UI setting applies to their user, not the tenant. In-tenant admin roles wait.
- Pull request 854 is already in production on release 116. That fact is settled. The empty prod counters are not.
