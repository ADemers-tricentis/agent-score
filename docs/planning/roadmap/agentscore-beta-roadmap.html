AgentScore Roadmap

Beta onboarding and self-improvement

Last updated 11-04-2026 · Horizons: Now / Next / Later

Target persona

A semi-technical user who can create agents but does not know how to configure traces, instrumentation, or agent updates. They create or evaluate agents and make the final call before an agent ships to production.

North star

Get as many users, including internal users, as possible using AgentScore regularly. The product must prove its value: users must actually use it and benefit from it. Onboarding should be so straightforward that the next action is never in question, and the user reaches a score they can act on as quickly as possible.

Target user flow

Steps 2 to 4 are Now. Step 1 (Trident sign-in) and steps 8 to 11 are Next, and steps 8 to 11 repeat until the score meets the ship bar. Steps 14 and 15 are Later. Internal coverage of Tricentis agents is Now and is not a numbered step. App usability, the JEV / decision model, and session grouping are Next and are not numbered steps. Steps 12 and 13, the previous self-improvement loop, are marked done and are not on this diagram.

Now

3 themes · 3 features

Improved onboarding

The first session, from sign-in to a first score, where every screen has one next action. Steps 2 and 3. The old Simplify onboarding feature list is marked done. This theme is the remaining bar.

Why it matters

The persona can create agents and does not know how to configure traces. A screen with several ways forward loses them before the first score. Self-service onboarding is a High ask from Wolters Kluwer and L'Oreal. Labs graduation criterion 1 is real active use, which depends on people reaching a score quickly. Open gaps remain: Home does not open the connect card, the first score does not start on its own, and verdict names differ by screen.

Goal: a user always knows the single next action, and reaches one score and one verdict for an agent they already run.
  • Guided first session Steps 2, 3
    Each screen offers one next action until the user reads a score and a verdict. No trace setup, profile picking, or eval writing on this path.
    Not re-checked against Jira

Workflow management

A multi-agent flow is shown as one workflow. Step 4.

Why it matters

Evaluating a group or hierarchy of agents is a High ask from Meta, Wolters Kluwer, Aptiv, Eaton, and Accenture. Aptiv asked to store and score a workflow as a whole. Accenture marked multi-agent workflow testing as priority 1. This theme is the showing and grouping.

Goal: a user who runs several agents together sees that flow as one workflow.
  • Workflow-level grouping Step 4
    Multi-agent flows are grouped and shown as one workflow.
    Not re-checked against Jira
Dispute risk: session grouping stays Next. It rolls one agent's traces into a task. Scoring or testing the workflow is a wider ask than showing the group.

Internal Tricentis agents

Every internal Tricentis agent is scored in AgentScore. An adoption outcome. It should not require AgentScore product work.

Why it matters

The north star is regular use, including by internal users. A full internal footprint is the proof that AgentScore is where Tricentis scores its own agents. It is on Now because it matters now and it does not wait on a product change.

Goal: internal Tricentis agents are connected and scoring, without a new AgentScore feature.
  • Score every internal Tricentis agent
    Rollout and adoption. No AgentScore product work is expected. The guided first session is the connection path, and that is separate work.
    No AgentScore product work expected

JEV / decision model

A hybrid scorer. JEV runs where it applies. The fuller judge runs where it does not. The point is less scoring time and less scoring cost.

Why it matters

Scoring cost and latency cap how often a user can run, and how many agents a tenant can keep on a score. A cheaper model on the cases it can decide keeps the fuller judge for the cases that need it.

Goal: each eval uses the cheaper model when that model is fit for the check, and the judge when it is not.
  • Hybrid JEV and judge
    Use JEV where it applies, and the current judge where it does not, to save scoring time and cost.
    Not re-checked against Jira
Dispute risk: what JEV expands to is unconfirmed. This card treats it as a cheaper decision model beside the current judge.

Session grouping

Related traces from one agent are grouped into a task or conversation, and the score covers that whole session. The per-agent scorecard is marked done.

Why it matters

A score on one trace misses a multi-step task. The August 24 working session called session-based scoring one of the two highest-priority product gaps. Workflow-level grouping is Now and is a different cut.

Goal: a user can score a whole task or conversation, not only a single trace.
  • Session grouping
    Related traces are grouped into a task or conversation, and the score covers that whole session.
    AI-15832

Tricentis identity

Sign-in through Tricentis identity (Trident). Step 1. This replaces Tosca Cloud sign-in. Probably not ready until Q1 2027.

Why it matters

Sign-in is the front door of the first session. Merito still has an open question on whether a Tosca Cloud tenant is required for auth. Trident makes the account a user already has the way in.

Goal: a user signs in with Trident and lands in AgentScore with one obvious next action.
  • Trident sign-in Step 1
    Tricentis identity replaces Tosca Cloud sign-in. Tenant login-or-create from the old sign-in stays shipped.
    Not re-checked against Jira
Dispute risk: this swaps the identity provider. It does not reopen tenant provisioning, joining a tenant, or AI Workspace tenant reconciliation.

Goal: every improvement carries a calculated cost or risk value the user can trust and compare.
